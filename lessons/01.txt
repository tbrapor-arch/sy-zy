# A word can force its reading: 只[ʦᴀʔ55]  (IPA with Chao tone numbers, _ between syllables)
title: 侬早 ！ | Good morning!
setting: A lane (弄堂) in Jing'an, nine in the morning. Sam has just moved in. The auntie next door is hanging out her washing.
--- lines
阿姨: 侬早 ！ | Good morning! | you early
Sam: 阿姨 侬早 ！ | Good morning, auntie!
阿姨: 侬 是 新[ɕin53] 来 个 伐 ？ | You're new here, aren't you? | you are newly come one, (question)?
Sam: 是个 ， 我 叫 Sam 。 | Yes. My name is Sam. | yes-it-is, I am-called Sam
阿姨: 侬 是 外地人 伐 ？ | Are you from out of town? | you are outside-place-person (question)?
Sam: 是个 ， 我 是 法国人[fᴀʔ55_koʔ55_ȵin13] 。 | Yes, I'm French.
阿姨: 侬 上海闲话 讲 得 蛮 好 ！ | You speak Shanghainese quite well! | you Shanghai-talk speak (it) quite well
Sam: 谢谢 阿姨 ！ 我 只 会 讲 一眼眼 。 | Thank you, auntie! I can only say a little. | thank auntie! I only can speak a-tiny-bit
阿姨: 再会 ！ | Bye!
Sam: 再会 ！ | Bye!
--- notes
1: 侬早 is "you early": the morning greeting, said until about ten. At other times people say 侬好, or just the person's title: 阿姨！爷叔！
2: 阿姨 is what you call any woman a generation older than you, not only an aunt. For a man the word is 爷叔 (lesson 2).
3: 伐 at the end turns a statement into a yes–no question, like Mandarin 吗: 侬是外地人 "you are from out of town" → 侬是外地人伐？ It is said with a low, short tone, vaq.
4: 是个 is "yes, that's right" (Mandarin 是的). "No" is 勿是 (lesson 3). 个 here plays the role of Mandarin 的.
7: Listen to the melody of a whole word. 上海闲话 zaon-he-ghe-gho is said with one tune: low on the first syllable, high on the second, then falling. The first syllable's tone decides the tune; the others lose their own tone. This is the main thing that makes Shanghainese sound different from Mandarin. Tap the word to see its contour.
7: 讲 is "to speak" (Mandarin 说). 得 links the verb to how well it is done, as in Mandarin: 讲得蛮好 "speaks quite well". 蛮 is "quite". 侬 is "you" for one person; for several people it is 㑚.
8: 一眼眼 is "a tiny bit": 一眼 is "a bit", and repeating 眼 makes it smaller. Nothing to do with eyes here.
