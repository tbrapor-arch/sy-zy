title: 吃 过 了 伐 ？ | Have you eaten?
setting: The next morning, at the breakfast stall at the end of the lane. An older man is frying buns.
--- lines
Sam: 爷叔 ， 侬早 ！ | Good morning, sir!
爷叔: 侬早 ！ 早饭 吃 过 了 伐 ？ | Morning! Have you had breakfast? | you early! breakfast eat (done) (already) (question)?
Sam: 还 呒没 。 搿搭 有 啥 ？ | Not yet. What do you have here? | still not-have. here have what?
爷叔: 有 生煎 、 豆浆 、 粢饭团 。 | Pan-fried buns, soy milk, sticky rice rolls.
Sam: 我 要 四 只[ʦᴀʔ55] 生煎 搭 一 碗 豆浆 。 | I'll have four buns and a bowl of soy milk. | I want four (measure) pan-fried-bun and one bowl soy-milk
爷叔: 好个 。 豆浆 要 甜[di13] 个 还是 咸[ɦᴇ13] 个 ？ | Sure. Sweet or salty soy milk? | soy-milk want sweet one or salty one?
Sam: 甜[di13] 个 。 | Sweet.
爷叔: 生煎 老 烫 个 ， 慢慢[mᴇ13_mᴇ13] 吃 ！ | The buns are very hot, eat slowly! | buns very hot (it is), slowly eat!
--- notes
1: 爷叔 is the polite word for a man older than you: the stallholder, the taxi driver, the man asking the way. It is also the word for a father's younger brother.
2: 吃过了伐？ "eaten (yet)?" is a greeting as much as a question, as in Mandarin 吃过了吗. Verb + 过了伐 asks whether something has already been done. The answer is 吃过了 "yes", or 还呒没 "not yet".
3: 呒没 m-meq is Mandarin 没有: "there isn't, haven't". 搿搭 is "here", 搿 being "this".
5: 只 is the measure word for buns, animals and many round things, said tsaq (Mandarin 个 / 只). Do not mix it up with 只 tseq "only" in lesson 1: same character, different word. 搭 is "and".
6: 个 after an adjective makes "a … one": 甜个 "a sweet one", 咸个 "a salty one", like Mandarin 甜的. 还是 asks "or?" in a question.
8: 老 before an adjective is "very": 老烫 very hot, 老好 very good. It has nothing to do with "old" here. The 个 at the end of the sentence adds weight: "they really are hot".
