Reading Shanghainese when every book spells it differently

Shanghainese has no official spelling. Each book you own picked, or invented, a romanisation. This app does not pick a winner: it stores every word once, as IPA, and writes it out in whichever system you choose in Settings. Pick the one your current book uses and the two will match.

The three systems in this app

Wugniu (吴语学堂拼音) is the most common online: Wiktionary, wugniu.com and most learners' materials use it. Qian's pinyin (钱拼, 上海话拼音方案) is Qian Nairong's system, used in his dictionaries and many printed textbooks; it is built on Mandarin pinyin. IPA is what linguists and the big reference works (《上海市区方言志》) use. The same words in all three:

Consonants: three series, not two

Mandarin has pairs like b / p (plain / breathy). Shanghainese has three: plain, breathy and voiced. That is the first thing that trips up a pinyin reader:

IPAWugniuQianPinyinExample
ppbb八 eight
pʰphpp票 ticket
bbbh(none)白 white
ʦtszz早 early
s / zs / zs / shs / (none)三 three / 是 to be
ɕ / ʑsh / zhx / xhx / (none)小 small / 谢 thank
ɦgh, y-, w-hh, yh, wh(none)鞋 shoe, 洋 ocean, 黄 yellow

So Wugniu b and Qian bh are the same sound, a real voiced b, while Qian b is Wugniu p. This is the main reason two books seem to disagree: they only use different letters. The voiced consonants (and ɦ, a breathy start before vowels) always come with a low tone.

Tones: five, and they mostly disappear inside words

A syllable said alone has one of five tones. Wugniu numbers them after the old Chinese tone classes, which is why they are 1, 5, 6, 7, 8 and not 1 to 5:

Inside a word, only the first syllable's tone counts: it sets the melody of the whole word, and the other syllables follow. 上海人 is not three tones in a row but one tune, low–high–falling. Tap any word in a lesson to see its tune. This is why many books do not mark tones at all, and why Wiktionary writes one number per word (⁶zaon-he-gnin) rather than one per syllable.

Old, middle and new Shanghainese

Books also disagree because the language changed fast. Older speakers keep more vowels apart (for example 麦 and 墨, or 打 and 党: Qian's new-speaker spelling writes both pairs alike) and up to eight tones; most people under sixty merge them. This app follows new urban Shanghainese, the speech of Shanghai-born people today, with five tones. If your book was written for older speech, some vowels will differ: that is a real difference, not a mistake.

When your book disagrees

Open the word's card and tap My book says…. Write the spelling from the book and its title. Your notes are kept in Settings, where you can save them to a file: they go back into the database and fix the word for every lesson.
